I am building a full-screen animated Paris wallpaper website. I want you to begin development of the project now.

This is a rebuild of an earlier concept. The priority is NOT simply getting something visible quickly. The architecture must be designed correctly around a layered scene system so that time of day, weather, seasons, lighting, aircraft, and special effects can all interact naturally without destroying image quality.

The visual target is a beautiful, slightly cartoonistic / painterly premium environment-art style. Think polished high-end game environment artwork: realistic enough to recognize Paris immediately, but with slightly softened and stylized shapes. Do NOT make it anime, heavily cartoonish, cel-shaded, or overly saturated.

The reference artwork I provide establishes the art style and Paris composition. Do not blindly copy lighting or colors from unrelated style references.

============================================================
CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE
============================================================

DO NOT build the Paris scene as one flattened image with aggressive CSS filters.

The wallpaper must be composed from multiple independent layers.

A full composite image may temporarily be used as a development/reference proxy, but the production architecture must assume separated assets.

The renderer must allow:

- time-of-day lighting changes
- weather changes
- season changes
- dynamic cloud coverage
- rain
- snow
- fog
- storms
- wet streets
- city lights
- monument lighting
- aircraft flying through the scene
- aircraft passing BEHIND the Arc de Triomphe and Eiffel Tower
- future special events such as a nuclear explosion
- smooth transitions between states

Do not bake clouds, rain, snow, fog, or other weather permanently into the primary scenery artwork.

============================================================
PARIS COMPOSITION
============================================================

The primary Paris scene should use this composition:

- Arc de Triomphe is a major foreground landmark near the center.
- Eiffel Tower is a major independently isolated landmark on the right.
- Parisian city buildings and boulevard occupy the midground/background.
- There is substantial open sky.
- Trees, boulevards, lamps, rooftops, and other Paris details should support the landmarks without overwhelming them.

The Arc de Triomphe and Eiffel Tower MUST be independent renderable layers/occluders.

This is important because aircraft must be capable of flying behind either landmark.

Do not simply draw aircraft on top of the completed scene.

============================================================
NO VISIBLE SUN OR MOON
============================================================

Do not render a visible sun or moon.

Pretend the sun/moon are behind the camera or otherwise outside the frame.

Their existence should only affect:

- ambient light
- directional lighting
- color temperature
- shadows
- sky appearance
- environmental illumination

This gives us more control over the composition.

============================================================
LIGHTING SYSTEM
============================================================

This is extremely important.

Do NOT recreate the previous mistake of putting multiple full-screen multiply/screen/soft-light overlays over the entire image until it looks like the user is viewing Paris through tinted glass.

The artwork should retain clarity.

Lighting should be subtle, layered, mask-aware, and derived from scene state.

Create a central lighting/environment state such as:

TimeOfDay:
- dawn
- morning
- midday
- goldenHour
- dusk
- night

Season:
- spring
- summer
- autumn
- winter

Weather:
- clear
- partlyCloudy
- overcast
- fog
- rain
- storm
- snow

Also support numerical values such as:

- cloudCover: 0–1
- precipitation: 0–1
- visibility: 0–1
- wind: 0–1
- ambientIntensity: 0–1
- lampIntensity: 0–1
- monumentLightIntensity: 0–1
- buildingLightIntensity: 0–1
- wetness: 0–1
- snowBrightness: 0–1
- hazeOpacity: 0–1

Derived lighting should come from time + season + weather rather than arbitrary component-level filters.

Lighting should affect individual scene layers differently.

Examples:

Midday:
- bright ambient lighting
- high clarity
- artificial lights nearly off

Golden hour:
- warmer stone/building highlights
- richer contrast
- warm but natural lighting

Dusk:
- decreasing ambient light
- streetlights activate progressively
- Eiffel/Arc illumination rises
- city windows begin illuminating

Night:
- dark but still readable
- city lighting, lamps, monuments and windows provide visual interest
- sky should not simply become a black overlay
- scenery should remain crisp

Overcast:
- softer shadows
- reduced contrast
- cooler/neutral ambient light

Rain:
- darker environment
- road wetness/reflections
- increased haze
- lights bloom slightly

Fog:
- depth-based visibility reduction
- distant city disappears before foreground landmarks
- lamps become more diffuse

Storm:
- dramatic clouds
- rain
- reduced light
- optional lightning illumination
- lightning should temporarily illuminate appropriate layers rather than merely flashing the whole browser white

============================================================
SNOW / WINTER BEHAVIOR
============================================================

Snow must behave naturally.

Do NOT use one bright white snow overlay during both day and night.

Daytime snow:
- relatively bright
- reflects daylight
- maintains surface detail
- slightly cool/neutral

Night snow:
- substantially darker
- unlit snow should be blue-gray / muted
- snow near lamps and illuminated landmarks may become warmer/brighter
- snowfall particles should also respond to scene illumination
- do not make nighttime Paris look like the ground is self-illuminated

Snow accumulation and active falling snow should be separate systems.

Season and weather must also be separate:

Winter does NOT automatically mean active snowfall.

Winter can be:
- clear
- cloudy
- rainy
- foggy
- snowing

Likewise snow accumulation can persist independently of active precipitation.
